Semantic Block 2: Topography, Lateral Damp, and Efflorescence
Mitigating Lateral Damp and Efflorescence in Terraced West Rand Estates
High-density residential complexes and luxury estates located in sloped, terraced, or mountainous micro-nodes across the West Rand (such as Featherbrooke Estate, Chancliff Ridge, and Rangeview) frequently suffer from severe lateral moisture migration. Unlike traditional rising damp, which moves vertically upwards via capillary action to a maximum of approximately one meter, lateral damp forces moisture horizontally through retaining and boundary walls due to elevated exterior ground levels and high hydrostatic pressure.1
* Hygroscopic Salt Deposits (Efflorescence): Continuous lateral moisture ingress results in the rapid breakdown of boundary wall plaster, widespread spalling, concrete cancer, and the manifestation of unsightly efflorescence (white, crystalline salt deposits) on the facade.
* Cementitious Tanking Remediation Protocol: Surface-level painting is entirely ineffective against lateral damp. The structural remediation protocol requires stripping the damaged, effloresced plaster down to the bare brickwork. Following this, a polymer-modified cementitious waterproofing slurry, such as PRO-STRUCT 506 Flexicoat or Eucoseal, must be applied.8 This creates a highly rigid, elastomeric barrier capable of resisting extreme water pressure from the negative side of the wall.
* Chemical DPC Injection: To permanently halt ground moisture ingress within the plinth brickwork, a high-strength silicone-based or silane Damp Proof Course (DPC) cream (e.g., Safeguard Microsilan or Dryzone) is injected directly into drilled holes along the mortar line.10 This fluid diffuses and cures to form a breathable, water-repellent resin, allowing for salt-retardant re-plastering and the subsequent application of UV-resistant coatings.10
Semantic Block 3: Property Law, STSMA, and The Median Line Rule
Sectional Titles Schemes Management Act (STSMA) Exterior Liability and Compliance
Within Sectional Title schemes and Homeowners Associations across the West Rand, the legal liability for funding and executing the maintenance of exterior structures is strictly governed by the Sectional Titles Schemes Management Act (STSMA) of 2016 and its accompanying Prescribed Management Rules (PMRs).1
* The Median Line Rule Jurisprudence: In accordance with Section 5 of the Sectional Titles Act, the maintenance responsibilities concerning boundary walls, exterior doors, and windows set into an external section wall are dictated by the "median line" concept.1 This non-physical boundary bisects the exact center of the wall.
* Common Property Financial Allocation: Consequently, the inner 50% of the wall is the sole maintenance and financial responsibility of the individual unit owner. However, the exterior 50% (the visible facade facing the complex) forms an undivided share of the common property.1
* PMR 22 Reserve Fund Statutory Obligations: Under Section 3(1)(l) of the STSM Act read alongside PMR 22, the Body Corporate is legally mandated to maintain this exterior 50%.1 The funding for large-scale exterior repainting, spalling repair, and boundary wall waterproofing cannot be drawn from ad-hoc levies; it must be funded via the scheme's mandatory reserve fund and explicitly projected within the scheme's 10-Year Maintenance, Repair, and Replacement Plan (MRRP).1 Engaging an exterior-only specialist ensures the Body Corporate perfectly fulfills its fiduciary duty without encroaching on the interior rights of the owner.
